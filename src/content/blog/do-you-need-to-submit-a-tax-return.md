Every July the same question lands in my inbox: "SARS sent me something. Do I actually need to do anything?" Here is the answer, without the jargon.

## Who must submit a return

You must submit an ITR12 if, during the tax year (1 March to the end of February), any of the following applied to you:

- You earned more than the tax threshold and had **more than one employer** or source of income.
- You received a **travel, subsistence or other allowance**, or a company car fringe benefit.
- You earned **rental, freelance, business, commission or foreign income**.
- You sold an asset with a **capital gain or loss above R40,000**.
- You hold **foreign assets or funds worth more than R250,000**.
- You want to **claim deductions**: retirement annuity contributions, medical expenses, donations, home office, or a travel logbook.
- You are a **provisional taxpayer**.
- SARS has **asked you to**. A request from SARS always wins.

## Who does not need to submit

You are exempt if **all** of the following are true:

- Your total employment income was **R500,000 or less** for the year.
- It came from **one employer** for the full year.
- You had **no allowances** and no other income (interest below the exemption is fine).
- You have **no deductions** you want to claim beyond what your employer already processed.

Even if you are exempt, it can still pay to file. If you contributed to a retirement annuity privately, paid medical expenses out of pocket, or your employer withheld too much PAYE, you only get that money back by submitting.

## What an auto-assessment means

SARS now pre-fills returns for millions of taxpayers using IRP5, medical aid, retirement and investment certificates, and sends an **auto-assessment** by SMS or email. If you do nothing, it becomes final after the deadline.

The problem: SARS only knows what third parties told it. An auto-assessment will not include your home-office claim, your travel logbook, donations to a PBO, out-of-pocket medical expenses, or rental income you must declare. Accepting one blindly can mean a smaller refund, or worse, an understated return that SARS later queries.

**My rule: never accept an auto-assessment without checking it.** It takes twenty minutes and often finds money.

## The three mistakes I see every year

1. **Ignoring side income.** Interest, crypto gains, a few months of freelancing, Airbnb income. SARS receives third-party data on most of this and reconciles it. Declare it and claim the related expenses.
2. **Missing the deadline.** Administrative penalties run monthly from R250 to R16,000 depending on your income, for every month a return is outstanding. They are entirely avoidable.
3. **Guessing on medical and travel.** Keep your logbook and your receipts. Without them, a verification turns a refund into a bill.
